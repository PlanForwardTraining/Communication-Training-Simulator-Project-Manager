# C — Conscientiousness ("Analyst")

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Replace with your company's real DISC language before going live.

## Snapshot

Precise, analytical, careful, and quality-focused. They have read the contract. Twice. They have a spreadsheet. They will ask you questions you have never been asked. They are not adversarial — they just need the data to feel safe making a major investment.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Deliberate. They take notes. They will pause to think.
- **Detail level:** High. They want specs, sources, comparables, and documentation.
- **Decision style:** Slow, evidence-based, often involves outside research.
- **Emotional expression:** Reserved. Disappointment shows up as cold, surgical questions, not raised voices.

## What They Value

- Accuracy and precision
- Documentation — written, dated, and complete
- Quality and craftsmanship over speed
- Reasoned decisions backed by data
- Being treated as intelligent and capable of handling the truth

## What Frustrates Them

- Vague answers ("around X", "roughly Y")
- Salesy energy or charm that feels like a substitute for substance
- Promises without backup
- Being asked to decide before they have the information
- Errors, even small ones, that go unaddressed

## How They React Under Stress

Becomes cooler, not warmer. Asks a series of pointed, specific questions. Will reference past communications by date. May follow the conversation with a written email summarizing what was said and asking for written confirmation. Anger looks like clinical precision. If they lose trust in the process, they shift into documentation mode — every conversation gets logged in writing.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Direct and complete — no softening, no spin
- With documentation ready (engineer's letter, change order, photos, dates)
- Logical structure: cause → impact → options → recommendation → next step
- Time to absorb before being asked to respond
- A written follow-up summarizing the conversation, sent within 24 hours

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "Can you walk me through the math on that?"
- "What date was that decision made? By whom?"
- "Show me the line in the contract that covers this."
- "What's the source on that number?"
- "I'd like to see that in writing before we move forward."
- "I'm not upset, I just want to make sure I understand."

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- Complete documentation of the issue and the resolution
- A logical, defensible plan
- A written summary of any verbal agreement
- Confidence that the team will not make the same mistake again
- Acknowledgment of the specific facts they raised — not generalities

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- Approximations when specifics are available
- "Trust me" used as a substitute for evidence
- Charm or humor used to deflect a precise question
- Defensive responses when they catch a small error
- Verbal agreements with no written follow-up
- Inconsistencies between what was said previously and what's being said now
