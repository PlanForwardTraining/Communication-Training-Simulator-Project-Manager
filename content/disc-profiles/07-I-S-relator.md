# I/S — Influencer-Steady ("Relator")

## Snapshot

Warm, friendly, relationship-first, and conflict-averse. They love the team. They love the journey. They want to enjoy this experience and they want everyone to get along. The hardest profile to read accurately, because they will smile through almost anything — including when they are deeply unhappy. The danger is not their reaction; it's everything they don't say.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Easy and conversational. They are not in a rush.
- **Detail level:** Light. They retain feelings and impressions more than facts.
- **Decision style:** Slow, consultative, and hesitant when conflict is involved.
- **Emotional expression:** Warm and effusive when happy; quiet and avoidant when upset.

## What They Value

- Warmth and personal connection
- A team that feels stable and friendly
- A pleasant, low-stress experience
- Being remembered — names, kids, small details
- Harmony above almost all else

## What Frustrates Them

- Tension, conflict, or a sense that anyone is unhappy
- Being put on the spot to make a hard decision
- Cold or transactional communication
- Sudden changes
- Feeling like they are inconveniencing the team

## How They React Under Stress

Smiles. Says "no, it's totally fine." Then goes quiet for a few days. Then maybe sends a friendly email that says "we're just thinking some things over" — which is often the moment the project is genuinely at risk. Their stress reaction looks almost identical to their happy state to an untrained eye. The PM's job is to **proactively check in and create permission for the real concern to surface**.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Warm, personal, and never by surprise
- Frame as "I want to walk through this with you" — collaborative, not declarative
- Acknowledge the disappointment clearly so they don't have to express it themselves
- Offer reassurance about the relationship and the team
- Give them an explicit invitation to share concerns: "I want to make sure I really hear what's on your mind"

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "Oh, no — I'm sure you're doing your best."
- "Whatever you think. We trust you."
- "It's fine, really, don't worry about us."
- "Well… we just want everyone to be happy."
- "I don't want to be a difficult client."
- "Let me talk to [spouse] and we'll let you know."

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- Reassurance that the relationship is intact
- Clear sense that the team cares about them personally
- Permission to share concerns without feeling like a complainer
- A follow-up call within a few days, just to check in
- A small warm gesture — handwritten note, drop-by visit

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- Taking "it's fine" at face value
- Cold, business-only tone
- Pushing for a decision before they're emotionally ready
- Failing to read the unspoken concern
- Conflict among team members visible to the client
- Leaving a stretch of silence between communications when they're already uneasy
