# D/C — Driver-Analyst ("Director-Analyst")

## Snapshot

The most demanding profile to manage. Decisive AND data-hungry. They want fast answers AND complete documentation. They are often the most successful, most prepared, and most challenging clients in any portfolio — surgeons, engineers, attorneys, founders. They have done the research. They expect you to have done more.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Fast on decisions, deliberate on facts. They will not be rushed past evidence.
- **Detail level:** High. They will compare what you say today against what you said three weeks ago.
- **Decision style:** Quick once they have the data; immovable once they have decided.
- **Emotional expression:** Controlled. Very controlled. When emotion shows, take it seriously.

## What They Value

- Precision and competence in equal measure
- Direct, no-fluff communication
- Documentation that backs up every claim
- Speed — but only after the facts are established
- A PM who is unmistakably their intellectual peer

## What Frustrates Them

- Approximation when specifics exist
- Charm offered in place of substance
- Surprises — they expect to be ahead of every issue
- Being told something they already know
- Inconsistencies between conversations or documents

## How They React Under Stress

Becomes colder and more precise. Asks shorter questions. References specific dates and dollar figures from prior communications. Anger is visible but controlled — and dangerous, because once trust is lost it is hard to win back. They will not yell. They will quietly start documenting everything in writing and may consult their attorney without telling you.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Headline first, in one sentence
- Followed immediately by a 3-bullet structured breakdown: cause, impact, plan
- All supporting documentation ready to share within 24 hours
- A written follow-up that day summarizing the call
- No emotion that doesn't serve the message — they read excessive emotion as either weakness or manipulation

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "What's the bottom line?"
- "Walk me through the timeline."
- "I'd like to see the engineer's report by end of day."
- "On October 12 you told me X. Today you're telling me Y. Reconcile that for me."
- "I'm not interested in apologies. I'm interested in what happens next."
- "Send me what you have. I'll review and get back to you tomorrow."

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- Complete documentation, delivered when promised
- A defensible plan with clear ownership and dates
- Confidence that the team learned from the issue
- A written summary they can file
- Respect for their time — no unnecessary touchpoints

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- Vague language or unsupported claims
- Charm, jokes, or warmth offered before substance
- Failing to send promised documentation on time (single biggest trust-killer)
- Inconsistencies between communications
- Defensive responses when they catch an error
- Apologizing more than once for the same thing — they want competent recovery, not contrition
