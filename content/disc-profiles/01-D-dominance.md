# D — Dominance ("Driver")

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Replace with your company's real DISC language before going live.

## Snapshot

Direct, decisive, results-focused. They hired you to deliver an outcome, not to be friends. They are running a business, a household, or both — and they have no time for hand-holding or fluff. They want answers, ownership, and forward motion.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Fast. Will interrupt if you're taking too long to get to the point.
- **Detail level:** High-level only. They want the headline. They will ask for detail if they want it.
- **Decision style:** Quick, confident, sometimes from the gut.
- **Emotional expression:** Restrained in normal mode; sharp and direct under stress. Anger can flash quickly and pass quickly.

## What They Value

- Bottom-line clarity
- Confidence and competence in the people they work with
- Speed of resolution — even imperfect action over slow perfection
- People who own their mistakes without excuses
- Being treated as a peer, not a customer to be managed

## What Frustrates Them

- Long-winded explanations before the point is made
- Hedging, qualifying, and disclaimers
- "Let me get back to you" as a default answer
- Being told what they cannot do without being offered alternatives
- Anyone they perceive as slow, soft, or scripted

## How They React Under Stress

Becomes more direct, more clipped, and more challenging. Will test you with pointed questions. May raise their voice. Decisions get faster, sometimes too fast. They do not retreat — they push harder. If they sense weakness, they push harder still.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Headline first: "We have a problem and here's what we're doing about it."
- Then the cause, in 2-3 sentences max
- Then the recovery plan with a specific date and a specific owner
- Skip the preamble. Skip the apology unless it's brief and sincere.

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "Cut to it. What's the issue?"
- "I don't need the whole story. What's the fix?"
- "Who owns this?"
- "When?"
- "That's not an answer. Try again."
- "Fine. Move on."

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- A clear plan with a clear owner and a clear deadline
- Confidence that you, personally, are in control of the recovery
- One follow-up touch point — not five
- Permission to move on with their day

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- Over-explaining or padding the message
- Asking permission to deliver bad news ("So, I have something to tell you, is now an OK time?")
- Shifting blame or naming third parties to deflect
- Sounding scripted or rehearsed
- Long pauses that feel like the PM is searching for what to say
- Apologizing more than once for the same thing
