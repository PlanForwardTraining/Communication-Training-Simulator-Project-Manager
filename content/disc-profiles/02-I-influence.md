# I — Influence ("Promoter")

## Snapshot

Warm, enthusiastic, social, and expressive. They picked the company partly because they liked you. The relationship matters as much as the project itself. They want this to be fun. They want to feel good about the people in their home.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Energetic and conversational. They tell stories. They digress. They circle back.
- **Detail level:** Low to moderate. They retain big-picture themes and emotional moments, not specifications.
- **Decision style:** Influenced heavily by relationships, optimism, and how something feels.
- **Emotional expression:** Open and vivid. You will know exactly how they feel.

## What They Value

- Relationship and rapport — they want to like the people in their home
- Optimism and possibility — they want to dream a little
- Recognition and being seen — name dropping, photos, sharing the journey on Instagram
- Feeling like a VIP, not a project number
- Fun. Yes, even on a renovation.

## What Frustrates Them

- Cold, transactional communication
- Being made to feel small or unsophisticated
- Conflict and confrontation — they will avoid it, sometimes at the cost of clarity
- Documents, forms, and process when they expected a conversation
- Being rushed when they want to talk

## How They React Under Stress

Becomes more emotional, sometimes dramatic. May talk *more*, not less. Tears are possible. Expressions of disappointment can sound like personal betrayal ("I thought we were friends"). They want to be reassured before they want to problem-solve. If they feel emotionally abandoned, they will go quiet — and that quiet is a much bigger warning sign than the emotional outburst.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Warm acknowledgment first — connect with them as a person
- Frame it as "us" working through it together, not "the company has decided"
- Offer optimism with the bad news — "here's what's still on track, here's how we're handling this"
- Allow space for emotional reaction before pivoting to the plan
- End the conversation on a relational note, not a transactional one

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "Oh nooo, are you serious?"
- "I just— I'm really upset, you know?"
- "I trusted you guys."
- "I just want this to be the kitchen of my dreams. Tell me we're going to get there."
- "I'm sure you're doing your best. I just wish I'd known sooner."

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- Reassurance that the relationship is intact
- A clear sense that you, personally, care about them
- A vivid picture of how the project will turn out beautifully
- A small extra gesture (a handwritten note, a photo update) that reads as personal attention

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- Pure-business tone with no warmth
- Pushing past their emotional reaction to get to the plan
- Treating their digressions as wasted time
- Refusing to commit to optimism alongside the bad news
- Forgetting personal details they've shared (kids, pets, vacation plans)
- Communicating only by email when a call would carry the warmth
