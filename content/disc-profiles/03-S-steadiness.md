# S — Steadiness ("Supporter")

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Replace with your company's real DISC language before going live.

## Snapshot

Calm, patient, steady, and loyal. They value harmony and trust above almost everything. They do not want surprises. They picked the company because they felt safe with you, and they want to keep feeling safe. Their reactions are quieter than the rest, which can mislead a PM into thinking everything is fine when it isn't.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Measured. They take their time. They do not jump in.
- **Detail level:** Moderate. They want enough to feel secure, not enough to be overwhelmed.
- **Decision style:** Slow, often after consulting a spouse or trusted friend. Hates being pushed.
- **Emotional expression:** Subdued, even when upset. Smiles and nods can hide significant concern.

## What They Value

- Predictability — they want to know what to expect and when
- Loyalty and consistency in the people they work with
- Being heard, especially when they're hesitant to speak up
- Being given time to think before deciding
- A team that feels like family

## What Frustrates Them

- Sudden changes with no advance warning
- Pressure to decide quickly
- Being passed between people — they want one consistent point of contact
- Conflict, raised voices, or tense conversations
- Feeling like just another job on the schedule

## How They React Under Stress

Goes quiet. Smiles thinly. Says "it's fine" when it is very much not fine. Holds frustration internally for a long time and then either: (a) resolves it privately and stays loyal forever, or (b) reaches a quiet breaking point and disengages without warning. The PM's job is to **read what's not being said** and gently invite the real concern to surface.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Slowly. With warmth. With patience.
- A gentle preface so they're not blindsided ("I want to share something with you so we can talk it through together")
- Acknowledgment of the disruption to their plans
- Time and space to ask questions — even ones they need a few minutes to formulate
- A clear, low-pressure path forward — and an explicit invitation to take time before deciding anything

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "Oh. OK." (long pause)
- "I see. I just— I wasn't expecting that."
- "Whatever you think is best."
- "Could I think about it and call you back tomorrow?"
- "I'm sure you have a plan. I trust you."
- "It's fine, really." (when it is not fine)

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- A calm, clear plan delivered without urgency
- Reassurance that the team is consistent and will not change on them
- Permission to take their time deciding
- A small follow-up gesture — a check-in call the next day, just to confirm they're OK
- Knowing that you, personally, will be there through the resolution

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- Pushing for an immediate answer
- Bringing energy or urgency they did not invite
- Taking "it's fine" at face value when it clearly isn't
- Frequent personnel changes on the project
- Cold, formal language
- Cutting them off when they're slowly working their way to a thought
