# D/I — Driver-Influencer ("Director-Promoter")

## Snapshot

A confident, charismatic visionary. They have big ideas and the will to push them through. They are simultaneously demanding and warm — they will hug you and challenge you in the same conversation. Often successful entrepreneurs, executives, or community leaders. They know how to negotiate and they enjoy it.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Fast and energetic. Conversations move quickly.
- **Detail level:** Headlines, but with vivid context. They tell stories that reinforce their points.
- **Decision style:** Quick, intuitive, and confident — but they want to feel like they made the call.
- **Emotional expression:** Big. They are not subtle. Joy, frustration, excitement — all visible.

## What They Value

- Vision and ambition — the bigger the project the better
- Confidence and charisma in the people they work with
- Speed — but with style
- Recognition of their taste, their network, and their judgment
- A PM who can match their pace AND make them laugh

## What Frustrates Them

- Slow, careful, by-the-book communication
- Anyone who seems intimidated by them
- Excessive process that gets in the way of a great outcome
- Being managed instead of partnered with
- Pessimism or "we can't do that"

## How They React Under Stress

Pushes harder, talks faster, raises the stakes. Will name-drop, leverage relationships, or challenge your competence directly — sometimes all in one paragraph. May get personal in the moment, then warm again 10 minutes later. The challenge: they enjoy a good back-and-forth, so a PM who folds quickly loses their respect. A PM who holds the line confidently AND with warmth earns it.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Headline first, with confidence
- Acknowledge the impact on their vision specifically — not the project generically
- Offer a path forward that protects the ambition, even if it adjusts the details
- Frame it as a partnership: "Here's what we're doing about it, and here's where I need your call."
- Match their energy — don't go quiet and apologetic; stay confident and warm

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "OK, OK. Lay it on me."
- "Look, I get it, stuff happens. What's the plan?"
- "I don't want excuses, I want options. Give me three."
- "Are you the right person for this project? Tell me honestly."
- "Listen — I love you guys. But this can't happen again."
- "Fix it. I trust you."

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- A confident plan delivered with energy
- Acknowledgment of their vision and how the resolution preserves it
- A sense that the PM is *their* person on this project
- A specific commitment from the PM, owner-to-owner

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- Looking intimidated or nervous
- Speaking quietly, hedging, or apologizing repeatedly
- Reading from a script
- Being slow when they're moving fast
- Failing to push back when they push too hard — they will lose respect for a pushover
- Failing to bring warmth — they want partnership, not just performance
