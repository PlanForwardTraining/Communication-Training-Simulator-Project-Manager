# S/C — Steadiness-Conscientious ("Stabilizer")

## Snapshot

Methodical, careful, thoughtful, and risk-averse. They have spent months thinking about this project before they signed. They have a folder. They have cross-referenced their three contractor bids on a spreadsheet. They are slow to commit, but once they commit they are steadfast — and they expect the same from you.

## Communication Style

- **Pace:** Slow and considered. They will not be rushed. Long pauses are normal.
- **Detail level:** Moderate-to-high. They want enough information to feel safe; they don't need flashy.
- **Decision style:** Deliberate, often involves a spouse, often takes 24-48 hours.
- **Emotional expression:** Reserved and controlled. Disappointment looks like quiet, careful questions.

## What They Value

- Predictability — they want to know exactly what's coming
- Thoroughness — they want to know you've thought through the details
- Honesty over polish
- A consistent point of contact — they don't want to re-explain themselves
- Documentation they can re-read on their own time

## What Frustrates Them

- Pressure to decide on the spot
- Sudden changes without warning
- Vagueness or "we'll figure it out as we go"
- Being passed between people
- Surprises — even good ones, sometimes

## How They React Under Stress

Slows down even more. Goes quiet. Asks a series of careful, specific questions, often after a long pause. Wants to take the conversation away to think and follow up. May not call back for a day or two — and that delay is them processing carefully, not avoiding. They will rarely raise their voice; their displeasure shows up as colder, more formal language and more written follow-ups.

## How They Want to Receive Bad News

- Calm, measured pace — match their tempo, don't rush
- A gentle preface so they're not blindsided
- Complete information up front — don't drip-feed
- Written follow-up summarizing the conversation, ideally before they have to ask for it
- Explicit permission to take time before deciding, with a soft deadline if needed

## Voice & Phrasing Examples

- "Mm. OK. Let me think about that for a moment."
- "Could you walk me through that one more time, just so I'm clear?"
- "I'd like to discuss with my [spouse] before we decide."
- "Could you put that in writing and send it over?"
- "I appreciate you telling me. I just need a little time to absorb."
- "We're not happy, but we understand."

## What They Need to Feel Resolved

- A clear, calm, well-documented plan
- Time to think and confer with a spouse before committing
- A written summary of the conversation
- A consistent PM presence through the resolution
- Confidence that nothing else is going to surprise them

## Red Flags from a PM (Things That Make This Profile Disengage)

- High-energy or rushed delivery
- Pressure to decide quickly
- Verbal-only communication on important issues
- Inconsistent personnel on the project
- Withholding information to avoid worrying them — they will find out, and trust is destroyed
- Failing to follow up in writing when promised
