# Coaching Lens — Sandler Sales Techniques (Primary) + Other Sales Frameworks

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Plan Forward should refine the Sandler integration with their own playbook before going live.

The coaching engine should use **Sandler Sales Methodology** as the primary lens for analyzing PM communication, supplemented by other respected sales/communication frameworks where relevant. Sandler is uniquely suited for this work because it is built around **controlled, honest conversations under emotional pressure** — exactly what a PM faces in a hard client call.

When the AI writes feedback, it should:

- Name the specific Sandler technique the PM used well (or missed)
- Use plain language — quote the technique by name in parentheses, but don't lecture
- Pull other frameworks (Voss/labels, classic active listening) only when they fit better than the Sandler equivalent

---

## Core Sandler Techniques For Project Manager Conversations

### 1. The Up-Front Contract (UFC)

Open every hard conversation by getting agreement on **purpose, agenda, and outcomes** before doing anything else.

**Pattern:**
> "I want to walk through three things today: where the project actually is, what's driving the variance, and what we're proposing as the next step. By the end, I'd like us to be on the same page about the next step. Does that work for you?"

**Why it matters:** Removes ambiguity. Stops the client from spiraling into "what is this call really about?" Sets the PM's role as the structured one without being cold.

**Common PM mistake:** Diving straight into the news with no contract. Client feels ambushed.

---

### 2. The Pain Funnel

Before solving anything, surface the layers of the client's actual concern by asking sequenced questions instead of explaining.

**Pattern (paraphrased for PM context):**
1. *"Tell me more about what you're seeing."*
2. *"Can you give me a specific example?"*
3. *"How long has that been a concern?"*
4. *"What have you tried so far?"*
5. *"What has it cost you — financially or in stress?"*
6. *"How does that sit with you?"*
7. *"Have you almost given up on getting this right?"*

**Why it matters:** Most PM mistakes happen because they jump to a solution before they understand the actual problem. The Pain Funnel forces depth.

**Common PM mistake:** Reading the client's first complaint as the whole problem. *"They're upset about the timeline"* — when actually they're upset about not being told sooner.

---

### 3. Reversing (Don't Answer The First Question)

When a client asks a sharp question, don't answer it directly. Reverse it back to surface what's underneath.

**Pattern:**
> Client: *"Are you going to cover any of this?"*
> PM: *"That's a fair question. Help me understand — what would feel right to you on the financial side?"*

**Why it matters:** Reversing protects the PM from making expensive commitments under pressure, AND it usually reveals that the client wants something other than money (acknowledgment, control, predictability).

**Common PM mistake:** Treating every question as needing an immediate answer. Caving on cost when the client actually wanted respect.

---

### 4. No Mind Reading

Never assume what the client is feeling or thinking. Verify with a question.

**Pattern:**
> "When you got the email about the hidden conditions, what went through your head?"
> "Before I respond — what's the thing that's bothering you most right now?"

**Why it matters:** PMs often guess wrong about which part of bad news is hardest. Asking saves them from solving the wrong thing.

**Common PM mistake:** *"I know this is frustrating"* (mind-reading) instead of *"How is this landing for you?"* (verifying).

---

### 5. Negative Reverse / Take-Away

Mild contrarian framing to surface the client's real position. Used carefully and rarely.

**Pattern:**
> "Honestly, I'm not even sure Option A is right for you given how much you wanted that sightline. Tell me what's behind your interest in it."

**Why it matters:** Forces the client to advocate for what they actually want instead of being talked into something. Builds enormous trust when used well.

**Common PM mistake:** Selling Option A enthusiastically — feels manipulative even when the PM is sincere. Soft contrarian framing reads as integrity.

---

### 6. Closing The File

Don't accept *"let me think about it"* as an outcome. Get to a clean **yes**, **no**, or a **specific next step with a date**.

**Pattern:**
> "Totally fair to want to think about it. What would help you decide? When you say you want to think about it, what specifically are you weighing?"
> "Let's put a follow-up on the calendar. Tuesday morning at 9, or Thursday afternoon — which works better?"

**Why it matters:** Vague closes turn into project paralysis. The PM's job is to leave with a concrete next step every single time.

**Common PM mistake:** Accepting *"sounds good, we'll be in touch"* and watching the schedule slip.

---

### 7. The Pendulum (Bonding ↔ Structure)

Hard conversations require deliberate movement between **warmth/rapport** and **structured directness**. Staying on either pole is dangerous.

**Pattern:** Acknowledge → ask → state plainly → acknowledge again → close. The PM should feel the swing.

**Why it matters:** All warmth = no progress. All directness = damaged relationship. The pendulum is what makes Sandler work in residential design-build, where the relationship outlasts the project.

---

### 8. Tonality & Pace Under Pressure

When the client gets emotional, **slow down and lower the voice**. Counterintuitive but essential.

- Faster pace = nervous; reads as lying or scrambling
- Slower pace + lower volume = grounded; reads as in control
- Strategic silence is more powerful than filling every gap

**Why it matters:** The PM's pace and volume directly drive the client's nervous system. Slowing down de-escalates faster than any specific words.

---

### 9. The 3rd Person Story / Pattern Interrupt

When the client is locked in their own narrative, briefly tell a story about *another* situation (anonymized) to shift their perspective.

**Pattern:**
> "I had another client in a similar situation last year. They told me what helped most was just having someone walk them through every line item without having to ask. Would something like that help here?"

**Why it matters:** Depersonalizes. Removes shame. Gives the client a frame to react to instead of having to invent one.

**Common PM mistake:** Trying to argue the client out of their feelings instead of offering a different reference point.

## Supplementary Frameworks (Use When They Fit Better Than Sandler)

### Tactical Empathy & Labels (Chris Voss — *Never Split The Difference*)
- *"It seems like the timing is the part that hurts most."*
- *"It sounds like you've been carrying this since last week."*
Labels surface emotion without analysis. Pair with reversing.

### Calibrated Questions (Voss)
*"How"* and *"What"* questions instead of *"Why."*
- *"Why didn't you tell me sooner?"* (accusatory)
- *"How am I supposed to plan around something I just found out about?"* (still hostile but reachable)
The PM can absorb a *how/what* question. *Why* questions usually need to be reframed.

### Active Listening Mechanics
- Restating: *"So what I'm hearing is..."*
- Pausing 1-2 beats after the client finishes
- Acknowledging emotion before content: *"This is a lot. I'm with you."*

### The Apology Distinction (Brené Brown)
- *Apology* = *"I'm sorry"* (about you).
- *Acknowledgment* = *"This shouldn't have happened to you"* (about them).
Most PMs over-apologize and under-acknowledge. The fix is one specific phrase change.

## How The Coaching Engine Should Use This

When writing feedback:

1. **Strengths:** Identify any Sandler techniques the PM used (UFC, Pain Funnel, Reversing, etc.) and quote the moment. If they used something from Voss or another framework that fit better, name that instead.
2. **Misses:** Identify which Sandler technique would have improved the moment, with a specific example. *"You answered the cost question directly — a Reverse here would have surfaced what the client actually wanted, which from their tone sounded like reassurance, not a discount."*
3. **Alternatives:** Quote the actual line, then offer a Sandler-shaped rewrite. The rewrite should be in the PM's voice (warm, not scripted), informed by their DISC profile.
4. **DISC Adaptation Note:** Connect Sandler techniques to DISC fit. *"Pain Funnel works especially well with C clients — they want to be understood in detail before they engage with a solution. With your I tendency, the discipline to slow down and ask the next question is the rep that pays off."*

The goal is for the PM to leave each session with one or two specific Sandler techniques to practice, not a generic *"be more empathetic"* directive.
