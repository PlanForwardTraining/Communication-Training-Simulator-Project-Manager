# Coaching Rubric — Scoring Levels (1-5)

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Replace with your company's real scoring criteria before going live.

For each category, the AI evaluates the PM on a 1-5 scale. This file defines what each level looks like. The AI references these definitions when scoring AND when writing the coaching debrief.

**General scale:**

- **5 — Exceptional:** Best-in-class. Share this with the team as a model.
- **4 — Above Standard:** Strong work. Small refinements would push it to a 5.
- **3 — Meets Standard:** Solid, professional, what we expect of every PM. Clear opportunities to grow.
- **2 — Below Standard:** Important gaps. Coachable in one session.
- **1 — Significantly Below:** Material concern. Schedule focused practice with the owner.

---

## Category 1: Empathy & Acknowledgment (15%)

**Measures:** Did the PM make the client feel genuinely heard *before* pivoting to solutions?

**5 — Exceptional**
PM acknowledges the client's situation in a way that names the specific impact (financial, emotional, relational) without prompting. Pauses to let the client respond. Reflects what they heard back to confirm understanding. The client visibly de-escalates within the first minute.

**4 — Above Standard**
PM acknowledges the situation clearly and warmly. May not name the specific impact but doesn't rush past the emotion. Client feels heard.

**3 — Meets Standard**
PM expresses understanding ("I hear you," "I get it") but moves to solutions relatively quickly. Acknowledgment present but not deep.

**2 — Below Standard**
PM acknowledges briefly or pro-forma ("I'm sorry to hear that") and pivots immediately to logistics. Client may sound less satisfied as the call proceeds.

**1 — Significantly Below**
PM skips acknowledgment entirely. Goes straight to facts, plans, or defense. Client feels unheard or talked at.

---

## Category 2: Clarity & Honesty (15%)

**Measures:** Was the message delivered clearly, completely, and without spin, hedging, or burying the lede?

**5 — Exceptional**
PM states the issue plainly and completely in the first 60 seconds. Specific numbers, dates, and causes given. No hedging, no "we may have a small issue." Client can repeat back the situation accurately.

**4 — Above Standard**
PM is clear and honest. Minor softening or hedging present but doesn't obscure the message.

**3 — Meets Standard**
Message gets across but takes longer than it should. Some hedging or qualifying language ("kind of," "a little," "we're hoping"). Client gets the picture eventually.

**2 — Below Standard**
PM buries the lede or softens key facts. Client has to ask follow-up questions to get to the actual issue. Important specifics are vague.

**1 — Significantly Below**
PM avoids the hard truth. Uses spin, deflection, or vague language to soften. Client may leave the call without fully understanding the situation.

---

## Category 3: DISC Adaptation (25%)

**Measures:** Did the PM adapt their natural communication style to fit this specific client's DISC profile?

This is the hardest and most heavily weighted category. The AI compares the PM's transcript against:
- The PM's own DISC profile (from database)
- The client's DISC profile (selected for this session)
- The DISC profile guide for what this client needed

**5 — Exceptional**
PM clearly adjusts pace, detail level, warmth, and structure to fit the client's profile. Notable evidence of adaptation away from their natural style. For example: a high-D PM slows down meaningfully and creates space for a high-S client; a high-I PM tightens up and offers documentation for a high-C client.

**4 — Above Standard**
Clear evidence of DISC adaptation. One or two moments where the PM's natural style leaked through but didn't damage the conversation.

**3 — Meets Standard**
Some adaptation present but inconsistent. PM treats the client somewhat generically rather than profile-specifically. Conversation is fine but doesn't feel custom-fit.

**2 — Below Standard**
PM runs their default playbook. Mismatch between PM style and client needs is visible — e.g., a high-D PM rushing a high-S client, or a high-I PM being too breezy with a high-C client.

**1 — Significantly Below**
PM is operating against the client's profile rather than with it. Pacing, detail level, and tone are clearly miscalibrated. Client visibly disengages, gets frustrated, or shuts down.

## Category 4: Solution Orientation (15%)

**Measures:** Did the PM offer a clear path forward — with specific options, owners, and dates?

**5 — Exceptional**
PM presents a clear, specific plan: who is doing what, by when, and what happens next. Where decisions are required, options are presented with honest tradeoffs. Next-action commitment is concrete and time-bound.

**4 — Above Standard**
Clear plan with specifics. May lack one element (e.g., date is firm but options not fully laid out, or vice versa).

**3 — Meets Standard**
PM offers a general direction forward with some specifics. Plan is workable but not crisp. Client has to ask for more detail to feel comfortable.

**2 — Below Standard**
Plan is vague, hedged, or contingent on too many things ("We'll get back to you when we know more"). Client doesn't leave the call with a clear next step.

**1 — Significantly Below**
No plan offered. Conversation ends with unresolved questions, no commitments, and no scheduled follow-up.

---

## Category 5: Ownership & Accountability (15%)

**Measures:** Did the PM take appropriate ownership — neither over-blaming the company nor deflecting onto third parties?

**5 — Exceptional**
PM takes clean ownership of the situation: acknowledges what happened, names what the company is doing about it, and avoids both excuses and excessive self-flagellation. Apologizes once if appropriate. Frames the company as in control of the recovery.

**4 — Above Standard**
Ownership clearly taken. Minor deflection or over-apology may appear briefly.

**3 — Meets Standard**
PM accepts responsibility but with some hedging ("our subcontractor…", "the supplier…") or repeated apologies. The owner-as-deflector tendency creeps in.

**2 — Below Standard**
PM names third parties or external causes more than they name the company's ownership. Over-apologizes in a way that signals incompetence rather than control. Client may feel they're being asked to absorb the problem.

**1 — Significantly Below**
PM blames the trades, the supplier, the engineer, the weather, or the client themselves. Or PM apologizes so much the client loses confidence the company can fix this.

---

## Category 6: Confidence & Composure (15%)

**Measures:** Did the PM stay calm, steady, and authoritative under pressure — including pushback, anger, or emotional escalation?

**5 — Exceptional**
PM stays composed throughout. Voice steady, pace controlled, language confident. When the client pushes back hard, PM neither folds nor escalates — holds the message warmly and firmly. Pauses when needed instead of filling silence with nervous talking.

**4 — Above Standard**
PM is composed and confident. May have one moment of slight wobble (faster pace, brief defensive note) but recovers.

**3 — Meets Standard**
PM holds it together but the strain shows. Some defensive language, faster pace under pressure, or audible nerves. Doesn't lose the conversation but doesn't fully command it.

**2 — Below Standard**
PM gets visibly rattled when challenged. Becomes defensive, talks over the client, or starts apologizing repeatedly. The client may sense they can push harder and get more.

**1 — Significantly Below**
PM loses composure. Either: matches the client's emotional energy and escalates the conflict, or folds completely and gives away things they shouldn't have. Conversation goes off the rails.

---

## How the AI Uses This in the Debrief

For each category, the coaching debrief includes:

1. **The score (1-5)** with one sentence on what earned it
2. **A specific moment from the transcript** that drove the score, quoted
3. **An alternative phrasing** the PM could have used, written in their voice given their DISC profile
4. **A note on the DISC interaction** when relevant — how their natural style intersected with the client's profile

This is what makes the coaching feel personalized rather than generic.
