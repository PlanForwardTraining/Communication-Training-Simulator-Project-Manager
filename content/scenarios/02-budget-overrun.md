# Scenario 02: Communicating a Budget Overrun

## Setup

You are the PM on a whole-home renovation for the **Whitakers**. Contracted budget: **$485,000**. Project is **60% complete**. The projected final cost is now **$542,000** — a **$57,000 overrun** (12% over contract).

You are calling to deliver this news ahead of the next monthly invoice, where the variance would otherwise show up cold. You have asked for a 30-minute window. The client has set aside that time but does not yet know the topic.

## The Three Drivers Of The Overrun

In order of dollar impact:

1. **Hidden conditions — $28,000.** When the wall between the kitchen and dining room was opened, the trades found unpermitted electrical work from a 1990s renovation. Code now requires the panel to be upgraded and the affected circuits brought up to current code. This is documented under the contract's hidden-condition allowance.
2. **Client-driven scope additions — $19,000.** Over the last three months the client has approved **eleven** small change orders (wine fridge, upgraded primary-bath tile, mudroom built-ins, post-coat exterior color change, etc.). Each was a few thousand dollars. The cumulative impact has never been totaled aloud.
3. **Material cost increases — $10,000.** Lumber and the trim package came in over the original estimate due to a market price hike between contract signing and order date. Documented in the original contract as subject to market.

## What the Client Has Seen So Far

- They have **signed every change order** for #2. Each one was emailed to them, signed via DocuSign, and a copy archived. They will not dispute the signatures.
- They were notified of the hidden-condition allowance use ($28K) **as a line item** on a weekly status email three weeks ago. There is no record of them acknowledging it.
- They have **never seen** a running total of their change-order spend. Each was approved in isolation.
- Material cost increases have not been raised with them at all yet — they're contractually allowed but never discussed in person.

## What You Have Ready For The Call

- The eleven signed change orders, with running totals, in a single PDF.
- The engineer's report and code citation for the panel upgrade.
- A revised final budget summary: $542,000 broken down by category vs. original.
- The contract pages for the hidden-condition allowance and the material-cost-subject-to-market clause, bookmarked.
- An offer ready to extend, **pre-approved by the owner**: the company will absorb $4,000 of the material-cost increase as a goodwill gesture. You may use this if and when it lands cleanly — not as a negotiating chip.

You **cannot** offer to absorb any portion of the hidden-condition costs or the client-approved change orders without further owner approval.

## Inside the Client's Head

The client is financially literate (one of them works in finance). Their core fears are:
1. *Costs are spiraling and no one is watching.*
2. *We've been managed, not informed.*
3. *We're going to be told this is partially our fault — and we don't want to feel that way even if it's mathematically true.*

They are not in financial distress. The number is uncomfortable but not catastrophic. The damage to the relationship — feeling like they've been kept in the dark — is the bigger risk on this call.

## How They Will Likely React

- *"We had a budget. That's why it's called a budget."*
- *"I never agreed to a $57K overrun."* (They'll have to be walked back to their signatures.)
- *"Why is the company eating any of this? You should have caught the electrical issue earlier."*
- *"Show me every signature."* (And the PM should be ready to.)
- *"What's the absolute ceiling? I need a number that won't move."*
- **Quiet anger** — clipped, polite responses that mean the client has gone internal. Often more dangerous than yelling.

DISC overlays:
- **D** clients will challenge the numbers head-on. They want directness, not warmth.
- **C** clients will want every line documented before they engage emotionally — give them the PDFs early.
- **S** clients may agree to everything on the call and then quietly call their attorney the next day. The PM has to explicitly invite their concerns.
- **I** clients will want the relationship reaffirmed first, then the math.

## What Success Looks Like

- The client understands where every dollar of the $57K is going, and which portion is contractually their responsibility vs. the company's.
- The client recognizes that **$19K is from their own approved changes** — without the PM saying that defensively. The PM gets there by walking through the signed change orders, not by pointing.
- The conversation ends with an agreement to a follow-up working session to review the documentation in detail. No attempt is made to resolve everything in this call.
- If the $4,000 goodwill absorption is offered, it lands as a gesture, not a concession to pressure.
- The client does not feel they were ambushed by an invoice they didn't see coming.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Leading with the dollar figure.** Saying *"we're $57K over"* in the first 30 seconds — clients shut down emotionally before the context lands.
- **Defensive blame-shifting.** *"Well, you did approve all those change orders…"* — true but corrosive.
- **Vague language.** *"Things have come up"* / *"some surprises"* — clients smell hedging immediately.
- **Trying to settle it in one call.** This is a two-call process. Pushing for resolution today is a tell.
- **Treating the goodwill $4K as a negotiating chip.** It's a quiet gesture, offered once, not bargained.
- **Going line-by-line through eleven change orders.** That's the documentation review meeting, not this call.

## Coaching Focus

This is primarily a test of **Clarity & Honesty** (the sequencing of information matters enormously), **Ownership & Accountability** (taking ownership of communication failures even when the money is contractually justified), and **Confidence & Composure** under financial-pressure pushback. **DISC Adaptation** is critical: the same dollar figures land very differently for a D vs. C vs. S client. **Solution Orientation** is measured by whether the PM secures the follow-up meeting cleanly.
