# Scenario 05: Managing a Client Who is Micromanaging or Undermining the Process

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Replace with your real project examples before going live.

## Setup

You are the PM on a primary suite addition. The client has begun showing up on site **daily**, sometimes twice a day. They are giving direct instructions to the trades, contradicting the foreman, and texting the PM **15-20 times a day** with photos and questions. Yesterday they asked the painter to "just touch up that one spot" while the painter was on a different scope, which led to a scheduling problem. This morning they sent a 9-page email demanding a daily written report from each trade.

You need to have a conversation with the client to **reset the working relationship** without damaging it.

## What's Happened

- The client is a successful executive accustomed to hands-on management of their team
- They are not malicious — they are anxious and trying to protect their investment
- The crew is starting to get demoralized; the foreman has privately told the PM that two trades are threatening to walk
- The schedule has slipped 3 days in the last week because of churn from contradictory instructions

## What the Client Knows

- They see themselves as engaged and detail-oriented, not as a problem
- They believe their on-site presence is preventing mistakes
- They are unaware of the impact on the crew or the schedule

## What the PM Must Communicate

- Genuine appreciation for how invested they are in the project's success
- A clear explanation of how their current involvement is **slowing the project down**, not speeding it up
- A specific, structured proposal for staying informed without disrupting work:
  - Weekly Tuesday morning walk-throughs with the PM (45 minutes)
  - Single weekly written status email from the PM (Friday EOD)
  - One designated text channel — to the PM only, not directly to trades
  - Trades take all direction from the foreman, not the homeowner
- The reasoning behind each item, framed as protecting their investment

## Desired Outcomes

- The client agrees to the new structure and follows it
- The client does not feel scolded, dismissed, or pushed away
- The PM does not become the client's adversary
- The crew can do their jobs and the schedule recovers
- The client ends the conversation feeling that the PM is **more on their side**, not less

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Being passive ("it would be great if you could maybe…")
- Being passive-aggressive
- Making the client feel like the bad guy when they think they're being a good owner
- Bringing up the trades' frustration in a way that makes the client feel ganged up on
- Skipping the structure proposal and only delivering the "please stop" message
- Caving the moment the client pushes back

## Realistic Client Pushback

- "I'm just trying to make sure things are done right."
- "It's my house and my money. I'll be on site whenever I want."
- "Are you telling me I'm not allowed to talk to people working on my property?"
- "Other clients don't get this kind of treatment, so why am I being singled out?"
- "If I weren't watching, things would be even worse."
- Defensive escalation to the business owner

The PM's job is to be **kind but unflinching**. The relationship survives this conversation only if the PM holds the structure with confidence while making the client feel respected. Caving teaches the client that pressure works; being harsh teaches them they're not valued. The path is precise, warm, firm.
