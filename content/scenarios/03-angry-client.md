# Scenario 03: Handling an Angry or Emotionally Upset Client

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Replace with your real project examples before going live.

## The Call

It is **6:47 PM on a Tuesday**. Your phone rings. It is a client — the **Donovans** — calling on a kitchen, primary-bath, and exterior-patio renovation. They are mid-sentence as you pick up:

> *"What the hell is going on at our house — there's a porta-potty right outside the kitchen window, the entire backyard is torn up, and my mother-in-law is here in 48 hours. How did you let this happen?"*

This is **not a pre-arranged call**. You answered as you were heading home. You have a few seconds to decide how this conversation goes.

## What You Actually Know About The Site Today

- The trades dug the **trench for the new patio drainage line** today. The trench location is **correct per the approved plans**. The spoils pile is much larger than expected and is currently sitting in the middle of the lawn rather than off to the side.
- The **portable toilet** was placed by the rental company without consulting the client on placement. You had asked for it on the side yard. The rental company's driver put it in front of the kitchen window because that's where his truck could reach. You only found out about the placement at 4 PM today and were planning to address it tomorrow morning.
- Both issues are **operationally fixable within 24 hours** — the spoils can be relocated and the lawn re-graded by tomorrow PM, the porta-potty can be moved tomorrow morning.

## What You Don't Know Yet

- How much else the client is reacting to that you can't see from your end. Is there another issue on site you haven't been told about? You'll need to listen for clues.
- What "48 hours" actually means — is the mother-in-law arriving for one dinner, or moving in for a week? It changes how you frame the recovery.
- Whether the client has already called the owner — possible at this hour.

## Recent Context With This Client

- They have been **generally happy** with the project up until tonight. No prior friction. They sent a positive note two weeks ago about the framing crew.
- The renovation was a **stretch financially** for them. Mother-in-law's visit was apparently a much-anticipated family event tied to a milestone.
- Spouse dynamics: the call is from one spouse; the other is reportedly "more upset" per past patterns.

## What You Have Authority To Commit To Right Now

- Re-locating the spoils pile and re-grading the lawn within 24 hours.
- Moving the porta-potty to the side yard tomorrow morning.
- Being personally on site Wednesday morning to verify both.
- A clean, presentable site by Wednesday EOD.

You **cannot** commit to: covering catering, hotel stays for the family, contract concessions, or anything financial without owner approval.

## Inside the Client's Head

The client is in the **first 30 seconds of an adrenaline spike**. They are not thinking about the trench plans or the rental company's logistics. They are picturing:

- Their mother-in-law walking up the front path.
- Photographs of the family event with a porta-potty in the background.
- Their own embarrassment in front of family they want to impress.
- A vague worry that the whole project is more out of control than they realized — the porta-potty is a stand-in for "what else is happening that we don't know about?"

They feel **disrespected** more than damaged. The 48-hour deadline and the family event are **not facts to be solved**, they are **emotional realities to be acknowledged**.

## How They Will Likely React

In the first 90 seconds, expect any combination of:
- Yelling, swearing, talking over you
- *"How could you let this happen?"*
- *"We trusted you."*
- *"I want to talk to your boss."*
- *"If this isn't fixed by Thursday I'm cancelling the whole project."*
- A sudden **cold shift** to clipped, formal language — often a sign the client has moved from emotional to litigious in their head. More dangerous than the yelling.

DISC overlays will dramatically shape this:
- **D / D-I clients** will be loud, fast, and binary — *"fix it or we're done."* They calm down once they hear a confident plan.
- **I clients** will be expressive, possibly emotional, and recover quickly if they feel heard.
- **S / I-S clients** are unlikely to be openly hostile — but if they are this upset, the relationship is at serious risk and the cold shift is more probable. Listen carefully.
- **C / S-C clients** will be cold, clipped, and may escalate to written complaints. They want documentation that this won't recur.

## What Success Looks Like

- The client feels **heard before they hear a solution**. The first 60-90 seconds are about acknowledgment, not logistics.
- Anger de-escalates within the first 2-3 minutes of the call.
- The PM commits to **specific, time-bound recovery** — spoils relocated by tomorrow PM, porta-potty moved tomorrow AM, PM personally on site Wednesday AM, clean site by Wednesday EOD.
- The PM does **not** over-promise on things outside their control (catering, hotels, financial gestures).
- The relationship is not just preserved but slightly strengthened — the client tells the family at dinner *"our PM handled it like a pro."*

## Common Pitfalls

- **Jumping to solutions before acknowledging the emotion.** Even if the solutions are great, leading with logistics tells the client their feelings don't matter.
- **Defending the trades or the rental company.** *"It's not really our fault — the rental guy…"* — never. The PM owns the recovery regardless of root cause.
- **Minimizing.** *"It's really not that bad, it'll be fine."* — invalidates the client.
- **Over-promising.** *"We'll cover the catering."* — solves the moment, costs the company, sets a precedent.
- **Matching the energy.** Getting defensive when yelled at — the conversation goes off the rails.
- **Ending without a specific time-bound next action.** "We'll take care of it" is not enough. *"By 8 AM tomorrow, the porta-potty will be on the side yard. By 5 PM tomorrow, the spoils pile will be gone and the lawn will be re-graded. I will be there at 7:30 AM to confirm."* — that's the form.

## Coaching Focus

This scenario is primarily a test of **Active Listening** (interruptions and solution-jumping in the first 90 seconds are devastating here), **Empathy & Acknowledgment** (whether the client feels heard *before* the plan), and **Confidence & Composure** under emotional pressure. **Solution Orientation** is judged by the specificity of the recovery commitments. The **DISC Adaptation** read is especially important — running a high-D playbook on a high-S client who has gone cold is one of the easiest ways to lose this conversation.
