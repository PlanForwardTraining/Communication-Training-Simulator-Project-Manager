# Scenario 03: Handling an Angry or Emotionally Upset Client

> **Status:** Placeholder content. Replace with your real project examples before going live.

## Setup

A client calls you, the PM, at **6:47 PM on a Tuesday**. They are furious. They came home tonight to find the entire backyard torn up — much further than they expected — and a portable toilet placed directly in front of the kitchen window with a clear sightline from inside the house. Their mother-in-law arrives from out of town in **48 hours** for a family event they've been planning for months.

## What's Happened

- The trades dug the trench for the new patio drainage line today. The trench location is correct per the approved plans, but the spoils pile is much larger than anticipated and is sitting in the middle of the lawn.
- The portable toilet was placed by the rental company without consulting the client on placement. The PM had requested it be placed on the side yard but the rental company put it where the truck could most easily reach.
- Both issues are operationally fixable within 24 hours but the client doesn't know that yet.

## What the Client Knows

- They walked into a scene that looks (to them) like total chaos
- They are imagining their mother-in-law's reaction, dinner photos, etc.
- They have been generally happy with the project until tonight

## What the PM Must Communicate

- Genuine acknowledgment of how upsetting this looks — without dismissing it
- A clear, fast plan to resolve both issues before the family event:
  - Spoils pile relocated and lawn re-graded by tomorrow PM
  - Portable toilet moved to side yard tomorrow morning
  - Crew leaves the site clean and presentable Wednesday EOD
- Confirmation that the trench work itself is on schedule and per plan
- A personal commitment that the PM will be on site Wednesday morning to verify

## Desired Outcomes

- The client feels **heard before they hear a solution**
- Anger de-escalates within the first 2-3 minutes of the call
- The client trusts that the PM will personally make this right
- The relationship is not just preserved, but strengthened — the client tells the family at dinner "our PM handled it like a pro"
- No promises are made about things outside the PM's control

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- Jumping straight into solutions before acknowledging the emotion
- Defending the trades or the rental company ("it's not really our fault, the rental guy…")
- Minimizing ("it's really not that bad, it'll be fine")
- Over-promising to compensate for the moment ("we'll cover the catering")
- Matching the client's energy and getting defensive
- Ending the call without a specific time-bound next action

## Realistic Client Pushback

- Yelling, swearing, or talking over the PM
- "How could you let this happen?"
- "We trusted you."
- "I want to talk to your boss."
- "If this isn't fixed by Thursday I'm cancelling the whole project."
- A sudden cold shift to clipped, formal language — often a sign the client has gone from emotional to litigious in their head

The PM's primary job in the first 90 seconds is to **listen, validate, and slow the conversation down**. Solutions come in minute three, not minute one.
